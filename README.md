# Type of Motion – _Experimental typography between body, movement and interaction_

Which movement creates which form? How does typography behave when it becomes fluid within a (digital or interactive) space and is in constant motion?

Typography moves beyond the two-dimensionality of paper or a screen. Instead, we explore it as a physical practice. The starting point here is not the finished letter, but the movement that creates it: a gesture, a line, a rhythm.

The body becomes the interface with the script, the tool, the surface and the interface for typographic experiments. From small finger movements in front of the screen to large-scale gestures with a brush and in space, we explore how movement can be translated into visual language.

The idea of encoding movements is not an invention of the digital age. Figurative alphabets, calligrams and body alphabets have fascinated people since the invention of movable type. From the 1940s onwards, dance scholar Ann Hutchinson Guest (_Movement Alphabet_) developed notations to translate movements into symbols.

However, movements can be captured and transformed into type not only using analogue methods and digital tools, but also through code and digital inputs such as body and pose tracking.

This gives rise to new forms of type—at the intersection of experimental typography, interaction, and visual research—that can not only be read but also experienced and brought to life. In this seminar, we will experimentally explore visuality and literality.

### Criteria

Course participation is assessed on the basis of the following categories:

- **Contexts / Concepts**: Depth and creative interpretation of the research
- **Create**: Diversity and ingenuity of experimentation, the creative quality of the designs and their coherent visualisation, handling and implementation of feedback
- **Tools**: Testing, applying and combining analogue and, where applicable, digital techniques, tools, programmes and machines
- **Skills**: Social interaction and communication, teamwork, active participation in exchange and discussions, development of visual competencies
- **Transfer**: Originality and quality of the final presentation, coherence and visual implementation of the process documentation
